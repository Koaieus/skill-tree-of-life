# Two axes of nesting — the cosmology the player pieces together

The fractal (`lore.md` → *The Fractal*) has always nested one way: a cleared
level condenses into a node of a bigger tree. The inner ring (#1651, #1652)
adds a second way in — your core holds a tree of its own. This doc is what the
two directions *mean*, what the player is shown of them over a run, and what
the game never says out loud. The final level that cashes it in — fought
inside the Apex's inner tree — is gameplay, and lives in
[`apex_inner_tree.md`](apex_inner_tree.md).

Everything here is lore and pacing of revelation; nothing is spec.

## 1. The owner's framing (2026-10-17)

Owner: *"the existence of an inner skill tree in your core is some kind of
hint about how this universe works"*. Clearing a level zooms the field out
until it condenses into one node of an *outer* tree; opening your core zooms
*in* to a tree. Is the condensed node a regular node, or was it the core
zooming out? The owner's own pro/con: yes, because that is already happening;
no, because one is procgen-rolled and the other static — *"but we CAN hint at
there being at least 2 axes of nesting … maybe clearing a level is a
node-zoomout and visiting your core's inner tree is a core-zoom-in & closing
it is some kind of zoom-out yet maybe we haven't _seen_ the core zoom-out from
your normal battlefield layer yet"*.

And the call that gives the second axis a destination — **settled (owner,
2026-10-17): the final level is a core's inner tree** (quoted in full in
`apex_inner_tree.md` §1).

## 2. The two axes

| | **Node axis — the world** | **Core axis — the self** |
|---|---|---|
| The move | field ↔ node | entity ↔ inner tree |
| Made of | procgen, rolled, *other* | authored, static, *you* |
| Crossed by | every Breakout: the cleared field condenses into your next starter node | opening your ring: you look *into* your core |
| Lossy? | **lossy** — compression carries stat and modifier, never edge structure (`lore.md` → *What Carries Forward*); the condensed node is a regular node | **lossless** — the ring keeps its *shape* every run; only the buys reset (`inner_tree_and_stake_economy.md` §AI and persistence) |
| Seen | outward, every level | inward only — until the end |

The core already crosses the node axis: it rides every Breakout carrying its
stats. What nothing has done yet is cross the *core* axis outward.

## 3. The unseen move

Closing your ring is the only zoom-out you have made along the core axis, and
it lands you back where you started — on the battlefield. So the open
question the second axis plants is the owner's: *we haven't seen the core
zoom-out from the battlefield layer*. If your core holds a tree, **what core
holds the field?**

The settled call answers it from the other side: at the penultimate Breakout
the player does not zoom out along the node axis — they fall *in* along the
core axis, into another being's core. The Grand Passive Tree is an inner
tree, and the ring the player has been buying all game is a miniature of it.
The ring's PoE look (#1652) reads, in hindsight, as foreshadowing, not homage.

**Why static vs procgen is the tell.** Every outer layer is rolled. The one
thing that never rerolls is what is inside you — and the one thing that never
rerolls at the top is the Apex's tree. The player who notices "the final
level is the same every run" has noticed the second axis.

## 4. The hint ladder (candidate, tentative)

A run should let a player who is paying attention assemble the picture, and
let one who isn't be surprised at the end — never told. A candidate ladder,
earliest first; each rung is a beat the systems already produce or nearly do:

1. **Level 5, the first ring point.** You open your core and there is a tree
   in it, drawn in the board's own node and edge visuals, with an immovable
   core at its centre (#1652 owner call: *"Graph but visuals and background
   and core visuals differ, core cant move etc …"*). The tell is plain: this
   looks like where you live.
2. **Every Breakout.** The zoom-out compresses a whole field into one node. By
   the third, the player has the node axis by heart — and it is the *only*
   axis they have seen move outward.
3. **The ring never rerolls.** Run two, same ring, same keystone spots. The
   fields changed; you did not.
4. **Bosses with sectors** (`apex_inner_tree.md` §5). A guardian's character
   reads as aspect sectors — the same vocabulary as your ring. Enemies are
   built the way you are inside.
5. **The penultimate Breakout zooms the wrong way.** The camera pulls back as
   always — and then dives. The player who had (2) by heart feels it before
   they understand it.
6. **The Apex's inner band is your ring.** Candidate (`apex_inner_tree.md` §4,
   Q-reuse): the innermost cells around the Apex's centre are laid out exactly
   like the player's own ring. The rhyme is the reveal; no text needed.
7. **The safe rhyme, true however the rest lands:** the Apex is a thick ring
   you grind arc by arc (`lore.md` → *The Ophanim — a ring you cannot cut*),
   and the player has bought their own ring all game.

## 5. What stays mysterious

Deliberately never answered in text — each is a reading the player may hold:

- **Whose core is the field?** If the Apex's level is an inner tree, every
  ordinary field might be one too, of a being too large to see. The game
  shows exactly one case and stops.
- **Are you someone's inner tree?** If the Apex is at the centre of its tree
  and you are at the centre of yours, the obvious next thought — that the
  player's core is itself a node in something's ring — is left for the player
  to have.
- **Which axis does the hub sit on?** The hub is a vertex with Tethers
  (`metagame.md` → *The hub is itself a vertex*) — node-axis language. Whether
  the metagame Breakout is a node-axis climb or a core-axis exit is the same
  open thread as `lore.md` Q9 / `metagame.md` Q8, and this doc does not
  resolve it.
- **Depth.** How many levels down along either axis the player started stays
  unknown (`lore.md` → *The Fractal*).

## 6. Open questions

1. **How loud is rung 6?** A literal match (the player's ring cell-for-cell
   at the Apex's centre) is a clean reveal but fixes the Apex tree's inner
   layout to the player's — and the player's ring is due to go per-CoreClass.
   Candidates: match the *default* ring; match the player's *current* class
   ring (the Apex tree's inner band swaps per player); match shape only, not
   content.
2. **Does the player ever see the battlefield zoom out along the core axis?**
   Candidate A: never — the absence is the mystery. Candidate B: once, as the
   post-Apex beat — the camera pulls back from the Apex's tree and it is a
   core on a field. Candidate B answers §5's first bullet; A keeps it.
3. **Is the condensed starter node "really" a core?** The owner's pro/con
   stands: lossy compression makes it a regular node. Leaning: keep it a
   regular node mechanically and let the player wonder.
4. **Does the Fairy know?** Whether the herald comments on the ring at its
   first opening, or stays pointedly silent, is a Fairy beat (`lore.md` Q6).
