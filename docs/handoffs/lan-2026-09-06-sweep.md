## 3. The melee / spike design pass — **this is what to pick up**

**#772** is the design session, now parented under **#170**. Two comments on it
carry the whole framing; read them before the session rather than re-deriving.

### The headline finding — and the correction that reshaped it

**First pass (still true as far as it goes).**
`BladePopResolver._reachable_from_pivot` is a BFS from the pivot over
`state.edges`, and `MeleeAttackPlan.get_induced_edges()` makes that edge set the
**induced subgraph** of pivot + members over the live graph. So cross-beams come
along automatically and **the player never declares a truss — selecting the nodes
is enough.** No new mechanic is needed to *build* a multi-spine blade.

`ClampAddon` braces go into `state.constraints`, not `state.edges`, so a clamped
spine is *rigid but still fragile* while a real cross-beam buys rigidity *and*
redundancy. **Real graph edges count for severance, phantom rigidity does not** —
principled, and easy to break by accident in a redesign.

**⚠️ The correction — owner, 2026-09-07 — which is the important part.**
I claimed vertex connectivity therefore protects a truss. It does not, because
**the swing rotates every spine past the same defender in turn.**

A spiked node is a point at a radius; the swing is rotational. With an unlimited
pop it takes *every* vertex whose arc passes it. So it does not contest a vertex —
it contests a **radius band** and deletes the whole **rung**. A 4-spine blade
severs exactly as completely as a 1-spine blade if the vertices line up radially.
**Spine count buys nothing against an unlimited defender.**

**What that proves:** the pop budget is not a nerf bolted onto the model, it *is*
the model. A per-node budget is what converts a radial rung-cut back into a
vertex-cut — and a vertex-cut is the only thing graph connectivity ever protected
against. Without it, blade architecture is provably irrelevant.

### The recommended rule: `b`, with `n` emergent

Give a spiked node a **pop budget `b`**; let `n` be the blade's spine count at the
contested radius. The intent is **severs iff `b ≥ n`** — `b = 1` kills a spine and
leaves a 2-truss standing, `b = 2` needs a 3-spine to survive. That reproduces the
owner's spine / truss / powerhouse gradient in one integer, and it is what the
boolean `get_spike_power()` should become now that the offensive half lives in
`blade_damage` modifiers.

**⚠️ Second correction — owner, 2026-09-07: most blades are not rigid.** They
*"scrunch up"*, kink, free-flop and whip at the tail, so contact is emergent XPBD
output. **There is no well-defined contested radius and no clean rung**, and the
`b ≥ n` table is therefore the **rigid-blade limiting case** — the right thing to
reason with, the wrong thing to implement. **Do not write code that computes
"spines at radius."** Implement `b` alone: a counter on the defending node,
decremented per pop. `n` is whatever the sim delivers.

**And the tension this exposes is the best thing in the design, unplanned:**

| Blade | Damage delivery | Exposure to a spiked node |
|---|---|---|
| **Floppy** | poor — energy lost into its own hinges | **low** — scattered arrival, few vertices meet one radius |
| **Rigid** (clamped / trussed) | good — energy reaches the tip | **high** — the whole rung arrives together |

Rigidity is simultaneously the payoff and the exposure. So a spike is not a wall
against all melee — it is a specific punishment for the optimised, whole-map-sweeping
blade, which is exactly the attack the owner said *"should be possible, but maybe
shouldn't succeed."* **The fix may need to be gentler than the rigid-case analysis
suggested**, since the catastrophic rung-cut is the price of a maximally-tuned
blade rather than the common case.

**Consequences:** blade-vertex HP becomes largely redundant (recommend dropping
unless it earns its place independently); *reduction instead of pop* is rejected
harder, since it has no `n` term at all and so cannot tell a spine from a truss;
and **radial stagger** becomes real second-order counterplay against spike
*density*, needing no new mechanic.

### The second defect: spacing luck

Vertices have circular hitboxes, edges have none and are inert for damage
(*"D-1 MVP: edges are inert"*). So a blade can thread *between* vertex hitboxes and
miss a spike entirely — the outcome turns on node spacing, which is invisible and
unchosen, and it cuts both ways.

**The preview does not predict pops** (owner: *"preview does NOT predict pop
(yet?)"*) — `MeleePreview` loops the swing sim for the ghost but never runs the pop
gate. **Do not plan around the data being one render away.**

**This is not optional work.** Because contact is emergent XPBD output, no rule,
tooltip or heuristic can tell a player what will hit — you cannot teach a rule for
chaotic output, only show the outcome of *this* swing. **The sim is the only
oracle**, so preview prediction is the sole mechanism by which any of this becomes
learnable.

But the precedent exists and it is not the player's: #498 step 3 made the *AI's*
estimate run that same gate against a shadow `CombatWorld`, so `AiCombatScorer`
already gets a prediction via `AttackOutcome.thinned_nodes`. **The AI can see what
the player cannot.** Closing that means pointing the existing shadow-world path at
the preview — and weighing the cost, since the preview loop rebuilds every cycle
and a resolve per rebuild is a per-frame shadow-world cost (lands next to #763;
likely wants caching on selection change).

### What is actually broken

**Nothing exhausts a spiked node** — it pops every vertex that reaches it, for the
whole swing, so the spike out-scales the redundancy for free and flattens the
gradient. Behind that: every contact is lethal (no HP, no partial outcome), and
none of it is legible to a player or to `AiCombatScorer`.

### Forks the session must settle

1. Pop budget: what number, per swing or per turn. If per turn, it interacts with
   `TurnManager`'s tie tick — first attacker eats the pop, second gets through
   clean. That is a coop coordination mechanic and a versus go-second advantage;
   intend it or key the refresh off something else. Also: what refresh means for a
   dormant-core owner.
2. Whether blade-vertex HP survives at all under `b` vs `n` — recommended for
   dropping. If it is kept, the scaler is a real question, and **blade degree is
   neither `get_graph_degree` nor `get_entity_degree`** — it is degree within
   `state.edges`, a third thing. Deliberately **not** written into
   `docs/domain/degree.md` yet, because nothing implements it.
3. What a spike's defensive half *is*, now that its offensive half is
   `blade_damage`. This is where `docs/design/skill_node_addons.md`'s standing
   **`FLAG` — Thorns vs Spikes, resolve before either ships** finally lands.
4. How any of it becomes visible — mid-plan to a player, and to `AiCombatScorer`.
6. **Do not tune `b` against AI-built blades.** They sit at the floppy end and will
   under-report how punishing spikes are against a well-built blade. Tune against
   hand-built blades at both ends — bare spine, clamped spine, rigid truss. The
   AI's own blade-*formation* gap is #771, widened for this.
5. ~~Whether braces stay out of reachability~~ — **settled**: real edges count,
   phantom rigidity does not. Write down the reasoning, do not re-open it.
