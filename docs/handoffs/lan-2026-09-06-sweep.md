## 3. The melee / spike design pass — **this is what to pick up**

**#772** is the design session, now parented under **#170**. Two comments on it
carry the whole framing; read them before the session rather than re-deriving.

### The headline finding, verified in code

**The topology gradient the owner wants already exists.**
`BladePopResolver._reachable_from_pivot` is a BFS from the pivot over
`state.edges`, so a blade's resistance to being neutered *is* its vertex
connectivity: a single spine is all cut vertices and severs catastrophically; a
2-spine truss is 2-connected and survives a pop; a 3-spine needs three coordinated
pops. Nobody has to design that.

**And `ClampAddon` braces go into `state.constraints`, not `state.edges`** — so a
clamped spine is *rigid but still fragile*, while a real truss buys rigidity *and*
redundancy. That distinction is load-bearing and falls out of the data structures.
**A redesign must not accidentally make braces count as edges for reachability.**

### What is actually broken

**Nothing exhausts a spiked node** — it pops every vertex that reaches it, for the
whole swing, so the spike out-scales the redundancy for free and flattens the
gradient. Behind that: every contact is lethal (no HP, no partial outcome), and
none of it is legible to a player or to `AiCombatScorer`.

### Recommendation on the table

**A pop budget per spiked node** as the spine of the fix, **plus #186** (free-flight
severed fragments, pulled into this pass and moved out of `Backlog`) as the
partial-success dial. Together: redundant blades survive intact, thin blades fail
*gracefully* rather than catastrophically.

⚠️ **"Reduction instead of pop" is recommended for rejection** — if nothing dies,
nothing severs, and spine and truss become identical. It is the one option
incompatible with the design the owner described.

### Corrections already applied, so nobody re-derives them

- A spike pop costs the attacker **output, not territory**. `land_on` returns
  before `super.land_on`, so the contact damages nobody; `dead_at` is swing-scoped.
  `blade_pop_resolver.gd`'s **own header comment still invites the wrong reading**
  and should be corrected whichever direction the session picks.
- `get_spike_power()`'s defensive use is an **early crutch**, per the owner — the
  offensive half already moved to `blade_damage` modifiers granted locally by the
  addon. Do not design around spike *power* as a defensive quantity.
- `melee-blade-sim.md`'s clamp section was stale (described a deleted
  `blade_clamp_constraint.gd` and an angular-range approach that never shipped).
  **Fixed**, with a note saying what it superseded.

### Forks the session must settle

1. Pop budget: what number, per swing or per turn. If per turn, it interacts with
   `TurnManager`'s tie tick — first attacker eats the pop, second gets through
   clean. That is a coop coordination mechanic and a versus go-second advantage;
   intend it or key the refresh off something else. Also: what refresh means for a
   dormant-core owner.
2. Whether blade-vertex HP is wanted at all once a budget + #186 are in. If it is,
   the scaler is a real question — *degree within the blade* is the cheap proxy,
   *is this a cut vertex of the blade* is the honest measure of blast radius.
   **Note:** blade degree is neither `get_graph_degree` nor `get_entity_degree` —
   it is degree within `state.edges`, a third thing. Deliberately **not** written
   into `docs/domain/degree.md` yet, because nothing implements it; add the line
   when something does.
3. What a spike's defensive half *is*, now that its offensive half is
   `blade_damage`. This is where `docs/design/skill_node_addons.md`'s standing
   **`FLAG` — Thorns vs Spikes, resolve before either ships** finally lands.
4. How any of it becomes visible — mid-plan to a player, and to `AiCombatScorer`.
5. Write down, deliberately, that braces stay out of reachability.
